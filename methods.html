How the estimates work

A guide to the numbers, the people they represent, and the places they describe.

The three key numbers

Current PM₂.₅-attributable deaths
Estimated annual deaths associated with outdoor PM₂.₅ exposure among adults aged 25+. The calculation covers non-communicable diseases and lower respiratory infections (NCD+LRI).
Avoidable deaths
Estimated annual deaths that could be avoided in the WHO-guideline scenario, using the same population and baseline mortality inputs.
Avoidable share
Avoidable deaths divided by current PM₂.₅-attributable deaths. It is not a percentage of all deaths. A share of 80% means that 80 out of every 100 modeled PM₂.₅-attributable deaths could be avoided.

Rates are deaths per 100,000 people in the corresponding age group. City rates and all comparison rates use adults aged 25+; these are crude, not age-standardized rates.

What does reaching the WHO guideline mean?

Each model grid cell with annual mean PM₂.₅ above 5 μg/m³ is reduced to 5 μg/m³. Cells already at or below that value are unchanged. This is a hypothetical exposure scenario, not an emissions-control plan or a prediction of next year's deaths.

The GEMM reference concentration is 2.4 μg/m³, so reaching 5 μg/m³ does not remove all modeled PM₂.₅-attributable mortality.

View the avoidable-deaths formula

In the current implementation, the avoidable fraction is (RRcurrent − RRscenario) / RRcurrent. It is multiplied by age-specific population and baseline mortality.

From data to a health estimate

We combine outdoor PM₂.₅ exposure, population and baseline mortality rates with GEMM to estimate health impacts in each grid cell, then combine the results for countries and city-centered areas.

View input datasets and calculation steps
  1. Exposure: ACAG V6.GL.02.04 annual PM₂.₅ for 2020–2023 is interpolated onto a 0.25° grid. Negative missing-value flags are excluded before interpolation. Source
  2. Population: GPW 2020 population and SSP1 2015 age fractions estimate the number of people in each adult age group. These inputs are fixed across years. Sources
  3. Baseline mortality: country-level, age-specific, both-sex GBD NCD+LRI mortality rates are used for each year. They are baseline rates, not downloaded PM₂.₅-attributable death counts. Cities use these national baseline rates. Source
  4. Risk: GEMM links long-term outdoor PM₂.₅ exposure to adult mortality; this implementation includes the Chinese male cohort. Reference
  5. Aggregation: grid-level deaths are combined for countries or city-centered windows. National mortality uses GPW population-weighted World Bank boundary fractions.

How is average PM₂.₅ calculated?

Country and city concentrations are both population-weighted: places where more people live contribute more to the average. Both use GPW 2020 total population of all ages, with the same population weights for every exposure year.

View the formula, population weights and missing-data handling

Mean PM₂.₅ = sum of (grid-cell concentration × represented population) ÷ sum of represented population.

  • Countries: represented population is the population assigned to each country using World Bank boundary population fractions.
  • Cities: represented population is the population within the city-centered 3 × 3 grid window.
  • Missing data: only cells with valid, source-supported PM₂.₅ and positive population enter either sum. A mean is unavailable if no supported population remains.

Health impacts are calculated from exposure in each grid cell and then aggregated. The displayed regional mean is a summary of exposure; it is not used as a substitute for grid-level exposure in the mortality calculation.

The age selector changes mortality results. Concentrations always use population weights for all ages, so they do not change when a different age group is selected. The two location types use the same weighting approach but cover different geographic areas.

Why do mortality estimates start at age 25?

The GEMM NCD+LRI parameter set and inputs used here cover adults aged 25 and older. Health impacts below age 25 are not included; this does not imply that younger people are unaffected by air pollution.

Country results default to 25–49, 50–69 and 70+, with 25+ shown as a separate total. Detailed results retain the original five-year groups from 25–29 through 75–79, plus 80+.

The 25+ total includes the smaller age groups; do not add the total to its component groups.

View how age groups are combined

Merged counts are sums of the source age-group counts. Rates and avoidable shares are recalculated from those sums and their corresponding denominators. The 25+ total must not be added to its component age groups. Minor differences can arise from rounding of source values.

City mortality results and comparison mortality values cover 25+ only. Parameter intervals are not calculated for the newly merged country age groups.

Nine cells around a city center

Each city result uses the model cell nearest the original city coordinate and its eight neighbors. The map's Show modeled area switch draws these actual grid-cell boundaries; the mortality circle remains a separate symbol.

A 3 × 3 window on the 0.25° grid covers about 75 × 75 km at 35° latitude. Physical area varies with latitude, and the selected window's area and population aged 25+ appear in the result card.

The window is not clipped to city or country boundaries. It can include surrounding communities, omit parts of a large municipality, and overlap other city windows. Do not add city results together to obtain regional totals.

Locations are selected from the SimpleMaps city source using a source population greater than 500,000; this source population is different from the modeled window population. Only windows with a represented population aged 25+ are included.

How to read circle sizes

Circle radii increase with the square root of annual avoidable deaths. This reduces the contrast between large and small counts so that smaller estimates remain visible. A minimum radius is also added; circle area is therefore not strictly proportional to deaths.

The scale uses the largest city estimate in the selected year and is shared across countries within that year. It can change when the year changes, so use the numeric legend and results to compare values. Circles mark city coordinates; the blue grid cells show the modeled footprint.

How to read the parameter range

The displayed ranges show how mortality estimates vary with GEMM risk parameters. They describe part of the uncertainty, rather than the full uncertainty in each estimate.

View parameter bounds and aggregation details

The source outputs contain a central estimate and lower and upper GEMM parameter-based estimates. The current Allcause code evaluates the central θ parameter and its sampled 5th and 95th percentile values. These are presented as GEMM parameter ranges, not a complete uncertainty assessment.

Country and city aggregation carries forward the source lower and upper fields. These should not be interpreted as a separately sampled probability distribution of each regional total. The merged age groups show central estimates only; no new interval is inferred by adding age-group bounds.

The ranges exclude uncertainty in exposure, population, baseline mortality, interpolation and geographic representation. They do not indicate variation from year to year.

Keep these limits in mind

  • Population stays at 2020 levels and age structure at 2015 levels. Annual differences reflect exposure and baseline mortality changes, not full demographic trends.
  • Results are modeled adult NCD+LRI mortality, not individual diagnoses, all-age mortality, or non-fatal illness.
  • Missing PM₂.₅ inputs are tracked; unsupported cells are zero-filled for health-model compatibility but excluded from the displayed concentration averages. Non-finite mortality cells are excluded from city sums without rescaling.
  • World Bank non-determined legal status areas are separate and unassigned in country aggregation. Where no regional total exists, the site provides available city results without inventing a total.
  • City results depend on the chosen window. Country and city results should not be treated as interchangeable spatial units.
